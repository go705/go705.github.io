Looking back on my journey from Taiwan to the United States for doctoral studies, I realize that God was not simply leading me toward an academic dream. Through years of setbacks, disappointments, and uncertainty, He was teaching me to rely on Him rather than on myself. I came to faith while studying in Taiwan, was baptized into the name of the Lord Jesus, later married in the Lord, and God blessed our family with two daughters. As a physics student, I was fascinated by the beauty and order of God's creation. After taking a course on Einstein's General Theory of Relativity, I dreamed of pursuing a Ph.D. abroad and one day becoming a professor.
In 2017, I began my doctoral studies in physics at the University of Arizona. I arrived in the United States full of hope, believing I was taking the first step toward that dream. Instead, I entered one of the most difficult seasons of my life.
---

## Learning My Own Weakness
Living in a foreign country brought challenges I had never experienced before. Although I had studied English for many years, everyday conversations were still difficult. I often misunderstood people or struggled to express myself clearly. Small misunderstandings slowly eroded my confidence, and I began to question my own abilities. 
Supporting a family as an international Ph.D. student was another challenge. Graduate funding barely covered our basic needs, and international students were not allowed to take additional jobs. We learned to live simply, cooking every meal and carefully managing every expense. Even attending church required sacrifice. The nearest True Jesus Church was about a two-hour drive away. Every Sabbath meant leaving home on Friday, staying overnight with brethren, and returning home late Saturday. Eventually, the long journey became physically exhausting, and I could only attend in person every other Sabbath. These challenges were manageable by themselves, but together with the pressures of doctoral research, they gradually became overwhelming.
Unlike undergraduate studies, doctoral research offers no guarantee of success. My research topic was difficult, and no one knew whether meaningful results could even be achieved. I spent countless hours reading papers, designing experiments, and searching for solutions, only to face repeated setbacks. My advisor was under tremendous pressure to produce results, and much of that pressure fell on me. His criticism was often sharp and discouraging. Although I tried to endure it, the constant uncertainty, cultural differences, and loneliness slowly wore down my confidence.
Then two major trials pushed me even deeper into despair.
---

## Trials Inside and Outside the Church
Around that time, I also faced unexpected struggles within the church. The church was made up of older members who had immigrated to the United States and younger members who had grown up there. As someone who had just arrived from Taiwan, I was already adjusting to a new culture and dealing with homesickness. Sometimes, however, casual conversations made that burden even heavier. People would occasionally remark that Taiwan was "strange" or that America was simply more "normal." Others joked that, as a physics student, I should know everything. A few even made inappropriate comments about my marriage.
Looking back, I do not believe most of these remarks were made with bad intentions. Yet at that time, when my confidence was already so fragile, they cut deeply. Because my English was still limited, I often could not express how I truly felt. The more criticism I experienced in the laboratory, the more sensitive I became to every careless word. Some older members also questioned why I only attended church every other Sabbath or why I left soon after services. They were concerned about my spiritual life, but they did not know the circumstances behind my decisions. Every Sabbath involved hours of driving, caring for my family, preparing meals for the coming week, and balancing the demands of doctoral research.
These experiences reminded me that, although the church belongs to God, its members are still growing in faith. We all have weaknesses, and sometimes even well-intentioned words can unintentionally hurt others. Through this experience, I gradually learned not to place my faith in people, but in God alone.
---

## When My Dream Fell Apart
The greatest trial came during the COVID-19 pandemic. Research slowed dramatically as laboratory access became restricted. Equipment frequently malfunctioned because repairs were delayed, and experiments that took weeks to complete often produced unusable results. Progress nearly came to a standstill.
Since research funding depended on results, my advisor became increasingly frustrated. During my fifth year, he told me that I was no longer qualified to continue my doctoral studies. He terminated my funding and instructed me to withdraw from the program. For an international student, this was devastating. Without funding, I could not afford tuition or living expenses. Losing my student status also meant I would soon have to leave the United States. After years of study, sacrifice, and perseverance, everything I had worked toward seemed to collapse overnight.
Friends encouraged me to move on and begin planning another career. Part of me wanted to do exactly that. I wondered whether this was God's way of closing the door. For many nights I wrestled with questions that seemed impossible to answer.
*Should I continue, or should I let go?*
*Was this dream ever God's will?*
The disappointment became so overwhelming that I shared many of my struggles in my Facebook post. In this post, I spoke directly and honestly about my frustrations with graduate school, the challenges of living overseas, and even some of the painful experiences I had encountered in church. As I looked back on my situation, I was reminded of Peter walking on the water. Many people focus on Peter's lack of faith, but what touched me most was his simple prayer when he realized he could no longer save himself.
> **"Lord, save me!"**  
> *(Matthew 14:30)*
I simply asked Him to give me the strength to endure whatever lay ahead.
---

## God Opened a Door I Never Expected
Although I prayed every day with my family, nothing seemed to change at first. Gradually, I began to accept that my doctoral journey might truly be coming to an end. As I prepared to leave the university, I met with friends one by one to say goodbye. During one of those conversations, a brother offered me simple but timely advice.
> *"Even if you're leaving, don't give up now. Finish well. Do your best to fulfill the responsibilities God has entrusted to you."*
Those words stayed with me. 
I realized that whether I graduated or not was no longer entirely within my control. But whether I remained faithful in carrying out my responsibilities—that was still my choice. So I returned to the laboratory. Even though I believed I would soon be leaving, I continued working every day, organizing years of research, documenting my results, and helping prepare my work for those who would come after me. I no longer worked because I believed success was guaranteed. I worked because it was my responsibility.
About a month later, something unexpected happened. My advisor called me into his office. Instead of dismissing me, he said, "I'll continue supporting your funding. Let's talk about how you can graduate." I was stunned. For years, graduation had never even been discussed because my research had made so little progress. I did not know what had changed, but I could clearly see that God had opened a door that I could never have opened myself. Even more remarkably, about a month later, after nearly five years of repeated failures, my experiments finally produced the results I had been hoping for. When I first saw the data, I could hardly believe it.
Looking back, I realized how close I had come to giving up. Had I walked away in anger, I would never have witnessed God's timing. Through that experience, God taught me a lesson that has remained with me ever since: Do your best to fulfill your responsibility, and leave the outcome in His hands.
---

## Learning What Truly Matters
About six months later, as I prepared to graduate, there was still one burden on my heart. I could not forget the day my advisor told me that I was not qualified to continue my Ph.D. Although our relationship had improved, those words had remained with me ever since. Before graduating, I finally gathered the courage to speak honestly with him. I asked, "When you told me I was not qualified and asked me to leave the program, I accepted it because I believed I had failed. But if that was true, why did you later allow me to graduate? Was I qualified or not? Even now, I still wonder whether I was truly capable." His response surprised me. He said,
> "Forget what I said back then. I was under tremendous pressure from the company funding our research, and I let that pressure affect the way I treated you. Don't hold onto those words."
Then he shared something that has stayed with me ever since.
> "What matters most is not what other people think about you. What matters is whether you faithfully carry out the responsibility entrusted to you."
Looking back, I realized there was wisdom in those words. Life is full of uncertainty. We cannot control how others judge us, nor can we guarantee success. What we can do is faithfully fulfill the responsibilities God has given us and leave the results in His hands. More importantly, I saw God's purpose behind those difficult years. 
He was not simply teaching me how to become a better researcher. He was teaching me to rely on Him, to recognize my own weakness, and to faithfully fulfill my responsibilities regardless of the outcome. By God's grace, my advisor and I were reconciled before I graduated. The resentment I had carried for so many years gradually disappeared. Then God opened another door that I never imagined possible. 
With my advisor's strong recommendation, I was accepted as a postdoctoral researcher at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT). This had once seemed completely beyond my reach. After years of believing I was a failure, I suddenly found myself at one of the world's leading research institutions. It was a reminder that our future is never determined solely by our own abilities. God sees far beyond what we can see.
---

## A New Beginning at MIT
Arriving at MIT was both exciting and intimidating. I was surrounded by brilliant researchers from around the world—people with extraordinary accomplishments and publications far beyond my own. It was easy to compare myself with them and wonder whether I truly belonged there. Supporting my family while adapting to a new research environment only added to my anxiety. During this time, one passage became a constant source of encouragement.
> **"My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness."**
>
> *"Therefore most gladly I will rather boast in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me."*  
> *(2 Corinthians 12:9–10)*
I gradually realized that much of my anxiety came from comparing myself with others. The world teaches us that we must constantly compete, because opportunities are limited and success belongs only to those who surpass everyone else. But as Christians, we are called to live differently. Jesus said,
> **"These things I have spoken to you, that in Me you may have peace. In the world you will have tribulation; but be of good cheer, I have overcome the world."**
>
> *(John 16:33)*
My value did not depend on being better than someone else. My responsibility was simply to do my best with the work God had entrusted to me. Whether He opened one door or another was His decision. There were still times when I struggled to surrender my own plans, but my prayer gradually became:
> "Lord, I will do my best to fulfill my responsibilities. Wherever You lead me, help me to follow Your will. When I am unwilling or afraid, please give me the strength to obey."
Ironically, accepting my own weakness brought me greater peace than striving to prove my strength.
---

## God's Guidance Beyond My Expectations
Although becoming a professor had always been my dream, I knew how difficult that path would be. Many outstanding researchers spend years applying for faculty positions without success. Some never receive an offer at all. I understood that this dream might never become reality. So while applying for faculty positions, I also applied for nearly one hundred industry jobs. I wanted to remain open to whatever path God might prepare. Surprisingly, none of the industry positions worked out.
Although I received interviews, every opportunity eventually came to an end. Instead of becoming discouraged, I took this as God's reminder to continue pursuing the path He had placed on my heart. Around that time, MIT offered a course on applying for faculty positions in the United States. A colleague encouraged me to attend. At first, I was reluctant. Eventually I decided to join, and it proved to be an enormous blessing.
The course taught us how to prepare research statements, develop long-term research plans, and navigate the faculty application process. We also learned from professors who had successfully obtained academic positions. Looking back, I can clearly see God's providence. Whenever I needed guidance, He quietly provided it through the people and opportunities He placed around me. As I submitted applications, I also prepared myself for disappointment. I prayed,
> "Lord, if becoming a professor is not Your will, I will accept it. My dream is important to me, but Your will is far better than my own."
With that mindset, I applied to nearly one hundred universities whose research interests matched mine. Then, to my great surprise, I received an offer from the University of Hawaiʻi. I never expected to receive a faculty position during my very first application cycle. Many of my friends and colleagues had stronger résumés and more impressive accomplishments than I did, yet they were still waiting for opportunities. At that moment, I was reminded of the words of Scripture:
> **"'Not by might nor by power, but by My Spirit,' says the LORD of hosts."**
>
> *(Zechariah 4:6)*
Even my doctoral advisor told me that it had taken him four years to secure his first faculty position. Human effort is important, but opportunities like these ultimately come from God. Looking back, I know this was not simply the result of my own abilities. It was God's grace.
---

## Looking Back
For many years, I thought God was leading me toward my dream of becoming a professor. Only later did I realize that this was never His primary purpose. His purpose was to transform someone who trusted in his own abilities into someone who would acknowledge his weakness and learn to depend on Him. Again and again, when I reached the end of my own strength, I found myself praying the same simple prayer:
> **"Lord, save me."**
Although I do not enjoy life's repeated cycles of hardship, repentance, and restoration, I have come to realize that this is often how God shapes His children. The Apostle Paul wrote:
> **"For I think that God has displayed us, the apostles, last, as men condemned to death; for we have been made a spectacle to the world, both to angels and to men."**
>
> *(1 Corinthians 4:9)*
Life has many seasons of success and failure, but these are only the changing scenes of the stage. What truly matters is whether we faithfully fulfill the role that God has entrusted to us. Looking back now, I thank God not because He allowed me to earn a Ph.D., conduct research at MIT, or become a professor. I thank Him because even when I no longer believed in myself, He never gave up on me.
Through every disappointment, every failure, and every moment of weakness, He patiently taught me what faith truly means. He taught me to rely on Him rather than on myself. He taught me that my worth is not measured by success, but by faithfulness. Most of all, He taught me that no matter how weak I may become, I can always cry out to Him:
> **"Lord, save me."**
May all glory be to the true and living God. Amen.
