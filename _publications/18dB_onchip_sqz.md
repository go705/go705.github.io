Squeezed states of light are essential resources for quantum-enhanced sensing, communication, and computation, yet achieving strong squeezing on integrated photonic platforms remains challenging. In this work, we demonstrate continuous-wave quadrature squeezing in a 1.6-cm-long adaptively poled thin-film lithium niobate waveguide with propagation loss as low as 0.1 dB/cm. Using a distributed loss-and-noise model combined with bidirectional second-harmonic-generation calibration, we rigorously infer an on-chip squeezing level of −18.06 dB and anti-squeezing of +20.64 dB, corresponding to a 95% confidence interval of [−18.96, −17.25] dB. These results represent the highest squeezing reported on any integrated photonic platform and establish thin-film lithium niobate as a leading technology for scalable continuous-variable quantum photonics, quantum sensing, and quantum networking.
